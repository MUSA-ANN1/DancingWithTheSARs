Dancing with the SARs

See where the land and the water changed, using NISAR radar, in a form light enough for a slow mobile connection.

A NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 project. The globe currently shows the Caspian Sea.

What is our problem

The Caspian Sea is experiencing a significant long-term decline in water levels, causing its coastline to gradually retreat. This is particularly concerning for the eastern coast of Turkmenistan, where the retreat of the sea threatens coastal ecosystems, wetlands, biodiversity, and human activities.

Despite the growing impact of this environmental change, monitoring its extent and understanding how the coastline evolves over time remain challenging. There is a need for accessible, reliable, and satellite-based monitoring tools that can visualize these changes and help people understand their environmental consequences.

Our goal is to address this problem by using Earth observation data to monitor, analyze, and visualize the retreat of the Caspian Sea along Turkmenistan's coastline over time.

Why we chose it

Radar works when cameras do not

Cloud, haze and night block optical satellites. Radar is not affected, so the same place can be compared reliably across dates.

The data is public but out of reach

NASA publishes NISAR products through Earthdata and ASF DAAC. The gap is not access to the data. It is turning it into something a person can read in seconds.

We can check it against places we know

Turkmenistan has a Caspian coastline. Starting there lets us judge whether a flagged change matches what is on the ground.

How we solve it

The heavy work happens on a server, not on your phone.

  1. Find the dataSearch NASA Earthdata for NISAR products over a chosen area and date range.
  2. Download on the serverFetch a capped number of products, so the device in your hand never has to.
  3. Pair two observationsAlign two images of the same place taken on different dates.
  4. Measure the changeCalculate the absolute change in radar backscatter on a log scale.
  5. Export and publish calculated results Export the processed results as a compressed GeoTIFF, a lightweight PNG preview, and a JSON manifest. Automatically upload the generated data to the project's GitHub repository under the dedicated data branch. The globe loads the manifest and preview first for faster visualization.
